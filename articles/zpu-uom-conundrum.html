The UOM Conundrum in IBP · Part 1

Meet ZPU: The One Dummy Record That Fixes Half Your UOM Problems in SAP IBP

The one interface record that shows up on every IBP project I've worked on. I gave it a nickname.

AR Anurag Rai · Published October 2026

Whatever IBP project you've worked on, you've hit this problem. And if you haven't yet, you will.

Across 10+ SAP IBP implementations — different industries, different planning scopes — there's one dummy data interface that I have to create every single time. It's not glamorous. It doesn't appear in any SAP best-practice guide. But it quietly saves the project from a cascade of UOM-related headaches. I started calling it ZPU.

The problem everyone knows but rarely discusses

If you've worked in IBP, you know this: when you open an Excel planning view, you can select only one Unit of Measure.

One. Not two. Not "base UOM per product." One UOM for the entire planning view.

Now think about what that means in practice. Your planning area has hundreds — maybe thousands — of materials. Some are measured in EA. Others in KG. Others in L, M, CS, PAL. When you open the Excel view and pick KG as your unit, what happens to the products whose base UOM is EA?

If the UOM conversion from EA to KG isn't maintained for that material — and why would it be, for a discrete electronic component? — the answer is: nothing. The cell is blank. The product vanishes from your planning view as if it doesn't exist.

The silent failure: IBP doesn't throw an error when it can't convert. It just shows an empty cell. Planners see gaps in their data and assume the numbers haven't been loaded yet. Nobody investigates, because there's no red flag — just an absence.

And it gets worse. This single-UOM constraint doesn't just affect what planners see in Excel. It ripples through the entire planning process:

  • Statistical forecasting breaks. Forecast operators in IBP require a UOM selection. If the selected UOM can't be converted to for a product, that product simply gets no forecast. No error in the application job log — it just gets skipped.
  • Copy operators and extracts fail silently. Copying actuals to a plan version or extracting data in a different UOM? Products without a valid conversion produce zero values or disappear entirely from the output.
  • Disaggregation becomes a nightmare. When aggregate numbers need to break down to product level, IBP uses UOM conversion factors in the calculation. A missing or zero conversion factor doesn't just block the product — it can produce divide-by-zero errors that halt the entire disaggregation run.

The fix: a dummy self-conversion record

The solution is almost embarrassingly simple. Create a dummy unit of measure — I call it ZPU (Z for custom, PU for Planning Unit) — and maintain a self-conversion record for every product where 1 Base UOM = 1 ZPU.

That's it. That's the trick.

ZPU doesn't represent a real physical unit. It's a pass-through. When you select ZPU as your UOM in a planning view, Excel template, operator, or data extract, every product converts successfully because every product has exactly one conversion: its own base UOM mapped 1:1 to ZPU.

The number you see is always the quantity in the product's own base unit. Sensor Module A shows 500 (meaning 500 EA). Polymer Resin X shows 2,000 (meaning 2,000 KG). They're in the same column, but each product is speaking its own language — and that's fine, because the planner already knows what unit each product is in or you can pull in Base UOM as attribute in the planning view.

Why this works: The real insight is that planners don't actually need every product in the same physical unit. They need every product visible in the same view. ZPU gives them that without forcing a conversion that doesn't exist.

  • Every product appears in the planning view. No more blank cells, no more phantom "missing data" investigations. The planner sees every product's quantity in its native unit.
  • Statistical forecast runs completely. Select ZPU as the forecast operator's UOM, and every product gets a forecast. The algorithm works on the base-unit quantity, which is exactly what you want.
  • Copy operators transfer all records. Copying actuals to plan, last year to this year, one version to another — all products come through because the conversion always resolves.
  • Disaggregation works without divide-by-zero. The conversion factor is always 1, so the math is clean. No zero factors, no skipped products, no halted jobs.
  • Interface extracts are complete. Extract in ZPU, and every product appears in the output. The downstream system receives the quantity in base UOM, which is what it expects anyway.

Setting it up in IBP

Setting it up is straightforward: create ZPU as a custom UOM (in IBP directly), maintain a 1 Base UOM = 1 ZPU conversion record for every product in the UOM conversion table via MDM rule or via CIDS Dummy data interface for UOM Conversions.

Then configure your Excel templates, planning views, and operators to default to ZPU. Planners will see quantities in base UOM without having to think about which UOM to select. It becomes invisible — which is exactly what good infrastructure should be.

Don't skip this: The number one failure mode with ZPU is a new product that gets created without its conversion record. Set up a validation check — a periodic job that flags any product missing a ZPU conversion — and make it part of your data quality monitoring. If it's missed, that product silently drops out of every view and operator that uses ZPU.

What ZPU doesn't solve

ZPU is a technical fix for the single-UOM selection constraint. It makes sure every product shows up and every operator runs. But it does not make it meaningful to add 500 EA of sensors to 2,000 KG of resin. The numbers appear in the same column, but their sum is still nonsense.

For aggregate-level planning — S&OP reviews, executive dashboards, portfolio-level demand shaping — you need a different approach. That's where revenue-based aggregation and UOM-group-based supply planning come in, which I'll cover in the next parts of this series.

But at the product level, for the day-to-day work of demand planners, supply planners, and integration engineers? ZPU is the most useful record you'll ever load into IBP.
